Trailmark UX review

Two separate reviews of Trailmark 0.47.73, one by Claude and one by Codex (neither saw the other's notes), read straight from the addon's code: layout, menus, settings, colors and how friendly it is to a new player. Both read the code, not the game, so sizes and colors are what the code sets. Each finding says who flagged it, and the last column is the verdict after checking the code.

ClaudeCodexBoth   HighMediumLow

Verdict

Guide window
Mostly good

Clean and compact, but the current step barely stands out, and the "Next step" arrow quietly marks steps done.

Menus
Mostly good

The gear menu is short now. The step menu shows "Open in quest log" twice and mixes safe and risky actions.

Settings
Needs work

11 pages, but one page holds about 25 options and several options live on the wrong page. No search.

Colors
Mostly good

All four feel like WoW, but three are dark with a gold accent and look alike. Tasks have no color language. Grey text fails contrast in two looks.

Friendliness
Mostly good

Setup and the tour are solid, but some helpers act on their own (restock spends gold, Fastest flips six switches) without saying so, and a few help lines are stale.

Do these first All built

  1. Make the current step unmistakable: bigger title and action line, later steps muted, lighter row tint.
  2. Stop surprises: the "Next step" arrow becomes a "Mark done" check, Fastest shows which helpers it turns on, restock appears in setup, the supply warning becomes a card instead of moving your waypoint.
  3. Fix the step menu: one "Open in quest log", grouped items, clearer names, Abandon last.
  4. Colors: one color per task kind in every look, a distinct accent per look, and fix every text color under 4.5:1 (prices on Quest Log included).
  5. Reorganize settings: split Finding the way, group the sidebar, one reward choice instead of two switches, add search.

5. One color language for every look

Today every objective uses a single "kill" color (Window.lua:74 maps all objective steps to kill), so "kill 10 boars" and "pick up 8 crates" look the same. Suggested: each kind of task gets one color meaning, the same in every look. Each look gets its own shade so it stays readable (4.8:1 or more on its background), but red always means kill.

MeansUsed forWhy this color
QuestAccept and turn inBlizzard's own quest yellow, the "!" and "?" players already know.
KillKill an NPC or mobRed is the hostile color on WoW nameplates.
LootItems that drop from killsOrange sits between kill (red) and gather (green): you kill, then loot.
GatherPick up from the world: crates, plants, oreGreen like herb nodes and gathering.
UseUse a quest item or click an objectViolet like the arcane item-use glow.
Go and talkTravel, talk, flights, hearthBlue like flight paths and the map.
TrainClass trainer, level goals, spellsTeal, kept apart from the travel blue.

Kept apart on purpose: quest level numbers keep Blizzard's difficulty colors (red, orange, yellow, green, grey), shown only on the level tag like [30]. Task colors go on the step icon and the objective line. The Icons page in Settings would show this table as the legend.

6. Make the looks different from each other

Cartographer, Waystone and Grove are all dark with a gold accent, so they read as one look in three shades. Suggested: tie each look to one real WoW material with its own accent. No gradients, no neon, no purple-to-blue. Each strip runs darkest to accent.

Cartographer
Navy and brass, the old login screen. Kept as is.
Quest Log
Parchment and dark ink, the classic quest log. Kept, with darker ink for green and red.
Waystone
Cold granite with a teal rune glow instead of gold, so it stops looking like a grey Cartographer.
Grove
Deep moss and lichen. Still soft and low contrast, but green all the way through instead of grey with sage.
Forge (new, optional)
Dark iron and ember: a warm dark look, nice for Horde and dwarf players.
